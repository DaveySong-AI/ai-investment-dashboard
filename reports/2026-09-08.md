# AI Investment Morning Report — 2026-09-08

> Research cutoff: 07:00 Asia/Shanghai. The latest completed sessions are September 4 for US equities and September 7 for Hong Kong equities. US markets were closed on September 7 for Labor Day.

## Confirmed facts

1. **Macro and US market** — August nonfarm payrolls increased by 162,000 and unemployment held at 4.1%, according to the US Bureau of Labor Statistics. On September 4, the S&P 500 fell 0.4% to 7,718.60 and the Nasdaq Composite fell 0.3% to 26,506.99.
2. **Hong Kong market** — On September 7, the Hang Seng Index fell 0.93% to 25,413.12. Tencent closed at HK$438.40, down about 1%, while Pop Mart closed at HK$156.20, up about 1.4%.
3. **Memory industry estimate** — TrendForce estimates total DRAM industry revenue rose 59.5% quarter over quarter to $154.73 billion in 2Q26 as contract prices increased and supply growth lagged demand. It expects conventional DRAM contract-price growth to moderate to 13%–18% QoQ in 3Q26.
4. **Pop Mart H1 disclosure** — H1 2026 revenue rose 23.8% to RMB17.17 billion and profit attributable to owners rose 10.1%. Mainland China revenue increased, but overseas revenue declined to RMB4.97 billion from RMB5.59 billion a year earlier.

## Analysis and judgment

- **Rate sensitivity is back in focus.** Stronger employment data do not mechanically determine Federal Reserve policy, but they reduce the market's comfort with a simple lower-rate narrative. That matters most for high-duration, premium-valued technology equities.
- **Memory is strong and still cyclical.** The DRAM revenue jump validates AI-server and high-value memory demand. It also raises the base for future comparisons and the incentive to migrate capacity. The correct follow-up is pricing, inventory, qualification and bit-supply growth—not extrapolating one quarter indefinitely.
- **Pop Mart's growth mix needs a closer look.** Consolidated growth remains strong, but the H1 regional split weakens the assumption that overseas expansion is already repeatable. The thesis stays on watch until overseas store productivity and cross-IP breadth reaccelerate.

## Fixed watchlist

- **AAPL** — The September 9 Apple event is a product catalyst, not yet an adoption or earnings result. Watch for concrete on-device AI utility and distribution timing; do not infer an upgrade cycle before evidence arrives.
- **GOOGL** — The long-term thesis remains enterprise AI monetization plus broad distribution. The September 4 decline does not alter it; cloud growth, AI monetization and regulatory exposure remain the key tests.
- **MSFT** — Azure and enterprise distribution remain the core strengths. The immediate risk is that higher discount rates and sustained capex intensity compress valuation before cash returns become clearer.
- **0700.HK** — The September 7 decline broadly matched Hong Kong weakness. Continue to test whether AI-enhanced advertising and cloud demand convert heavy infrastructure spending into durable cash returns.
- **9992.HK** — H1 total growth is confirmed, but overseas revenue declined year over year. This is now the clearest falsifiable checkpoint for the global-IP thesis.
- **DRAM.A / Roundhill DRAM ETF context** — Treat the instrument as actively managed global memory-equity exposure, not a spot DRAM price index. The positive industry estimate strengthens the current demand signal but does not remove holdings, valuation, FX, capacity or cycle risk.

## Watch next

- The first US cash-equity session after Labor Day: whether yields and premium technology valuations stabilize.
- Apple's September 9 event: separate announced features from ship dates, device eligibility and observable adoption.
- Conventional DRAM contract-price momentum, HBM qualification and suppliers' advanced-node migration through 3Q26.
- Pop Mart's overseas revenue and store productivity in the next business update.
